# Meridian Bank · Procurement review — 2026-08-12

## Headline state of play

Procurement has taken the pen from the business. The Head of Digital Channels still owns the
outcome and repeated that the v1 scope is the scope they want, but the commercial conversation now
runs through Procurement, who opened by asking for 12% off the anchored ACV as the condition for
moving to a shortlist of one. Two things moved in our favour in the same meeting and neither was
priced: they revised active-user volumes up from 150,000 to 185,000, and their Enterprise Architect
confirmed core-integration readiness has slipped two quarters, from Q1 2027 to Q3 2027. The deal is
not at risk; the *shape* of the next move is the whole question.

## What was covered / demonstrated

- Walkthrough of the v1 proposal's two scenarios — Best (full scope, 5-yr term) and Better
  (phase 2 deferred) — with the published volume-tier table shown on screen.
- Procurement's benchmark pack: three unnamed "comparable platform deals" they say sit 10–15%
  below our anchored per-user rate. No sources, no scope definitions, not shared with us.
- Revised volume plan from the Head of Digital Channels: 185,000 active users at go-live-plus-12,
  up from the 150,000 used in the v1 model, driven by the small-business segment migrating earlier
  than assumed.
- Enterprise Architect's integration status: the core-banking API programme has slipped two
  quarters. Their own readiness date is now Q3 2027, against the Q1 2027 assumed in the v1 plan.

## Key exchanges & tensions

**Price — the 12% ask.**
*Procurement position:* "Twelve percent off the annual number and we can go to a single-vendor
recommendation this quarter." Framed as a threshold, not an opening.
*Our position:* No number given in the room. We restated that the anchor is full-scope and
list-anchored, and that any movement is traded, not granted.
*Resolution:* **Open.** We committed to come back with a structured response, not a counter-number,
by 2026-08-26. Nothing was conceded verbally.

**Volume — 150,000 → 185,000 active users.**
*Client position:* Presented as a reason the per-user rate should fall — "we're bringing you more
users, that should be worth something."
*Our position:* Noted that the published tier table already prices that: at 185,000 the applicable
published rate is the second tier rather than the first, so the step-down is automatic and visible
to them. Restated the base fee is unchanged.
*Resolution:* Accepted in the room, without argument. The Head of Digital Channels confirmed the
185,000 figure is a planning number their COO has signed, not a hope.

**Integration readiness — two-quarter slip.**
*Client position:* Raised by the Enterprise Architect as a risk disclosure, not as a commercial ask
— he was explicit that "this is our delay, not yours."
*Our position:* We asked only clarifying questions: which dependencies, whether Q3 2027 is
committed or indicative, and whether it affects the whole scope or the origination journeys only.
Answer: the origination journeys only; the servicing scope is unaffected and can start on the
original date.
*Resolution:* Confirmed as fact. **Not yet converted into anything commercial** — we made no offer
against it in the room, deliberately.

**Who owns the decision.**
*Tension:* Procurement described themselves as "the process owner from here"; the Head of Digital
Channels twice restated the business requirement mid-meeting. The Enterprise Architect deferred to
Digital Channels on scope, to Procurement on commercials.
*Resolution:* Open. No formal statement of the decision path was given, and we did not press for it
in front of Procurement.

## Strategic reads

- **The 12% ask is a threshold claim, not a floor.** It arrived without scope definitions and
  without a comparable, and it was made in the same meeting they told us their volume plan grew.
  Buyers who intend to walk do not expand the scope they are walking away from. Treat it as an
  opening posture and respond structurally.
- **Their delay is our cheapest give.** A two-quarter readiness slip that they volunteered as their
  own risk hands us timing and phasing levers we did not have at round 1 — staggered activation and
  a stub bill cost us cost-of-capital, not margin. Matching a price ask with a timing answer is the
  whole point of spending families 1→4 before family 5.
- **Do not pay twice for the volume increase.** The published tier table already steps the rate down
  at 185,000. If we also discount, we have funded the same concession twice and set the renewal
  reference at the lower number. Show them the tier arithmetic instead — it is a concession they can
  see and it costs us nothing beyond list.
- **Procurement is being used as the pressure channel, not the decision-maker.** The business owner
  corrected the scope framing twice in front of them. The risk is not losing on price; it is letting
  the commercial conversation drift away from the person who owns the outcome. Protect the Head of
  Digital Channels — do not make them defend our number to their own Procurement team.
- **The unscoped benchmark pack is a soft spot we should test once, politely.** Asking for the scope
  definitions behind their comparators is a reasonable request that either produces something we can
  answer or quietly removes the comparison from the table.
- **Watch the shape, not just the number.** Any move at round 2 becomes the size the buyer expects at
  round 3. A large move here leaves nothing credible for the closing round.
